### 1. Mehrgarh Civilization (~7000 BCE – 2600 BCE)

* **Location:** Located at the foot of the Bolan Pass on the Kacchi Plain of Balochistan. Discovered in 1974 by French archaeologist Jean-François Jarrige.
* **Significance:** Proved that urbanism in the Indus Valley was **indigenous** and not imported from Mesopotamia.
* **Key Features:**
* **Earliest Agrarian Settlement:** Early evidence of wheat/barley farming and zebu cattle domestication in South Asia.
* **Proto-Dentistry:** Archaeologists discovered 9,000-year-old human teeth showing evidence of drilling with stone tools.
* **Craftsmanship:** Mud-brick dwellings, terracotta female figurines ("Mother Goddess"), and early metallurgy (copper beads).



---

### 2. Kot Diji Civilization (~3300 BCE – 2600 BCE)

* **Location:** Situated in the Khairpur District of Sindh, near the east bank of the Indus River. Excavated by Dr. F.A. Khan in 1955.
* **Significance:** Represents the **Early Harappan / Formative Phase** of the Indus Valley Civilization.
* **Key Features:**
* **Fortified Town:** Earliest defensive stone-foundation mud-brick walls and citadels.
* **Kot Dijian Pottery:** Distinctive wheel-made pottery featuring dark rim bands and fine geometric/fish-scale painting.
* **Urban Transition:** Provided the structural layout, standardized brick ratios, and kiln technologies later perfected at Mohenjo-Daro and Harappa.



---

### 3. Indus Valley Civilization (IVC) (~2600 BCE – 1900 BCE)

The **Mature Harappan Phase** represents one of the world's three earliest great civilizations (alongside Egypt and Mesopotamia), stretching across the Indus River basin.

* **Core Characteristics:**
* **Town Planning:** Grid-pattern streets, multi-story mud-brick houses, standard baked bricks (1:2:4 ratio), and underground drainage systems.
* **Economy & Trade:** Agricultural surplus (granaries), weight standardization, and extensive maritime trade with Mesopotamia (referred to as *Meluhha*).
* **Script & Seals:** Steatite seals featuring animal motifs (Unicorn, Bull, Elephant) and undeciphered Indus script.



#### Major Cities:

1. **Mohenjo-Daro ("Mound of the Dead"):**
* Located in Larkana District, Sindh. Discovered by R.D. Banerji in 1922.
* Famous for the **Great Bath**, public granary, **Dancing Girl** bronze statue, and the **Priest-King** bust.


2. **Harappa:**
* Located in Sahiwal District, Punjab. Excavated by Daya Ram Sahni in 1921.
* Famous for the Citadel mound, row of granaries near the Ravi River, and complex craft workshops.

### 4. Gandhara Civilization (~1000 BCE – 500 CE)

* **Location:** Concentrated in Northern Pakistan (Peshawar Valley, Swat, Mardan, and Taxila).
* **Significance:** A major crossroads where Greco-Roman artistic influences merged with Indian Buddhist traditions under ruler dynasties like the **Kushans** (most notably King Kanishka).
* **Key Features:**
* **Greco-Buddhist Art:** Unique sculptures depicting the Buddha with realism, wavy hair, and toga-like drapery inspired by Greek sculpture.
* **Taxila (Takshashila):** A UNESCO World Heritage site and ancient global center of learning, philosophy, medicine, and diplomacy (notably associated with Chanakya/Kautilya).
* **Monastic Complexes:** Prominent sites include **Takht-i-Bahi** (Mardan), **Dharmarajika Stupa** (Taxila), and **Jamal Garhi**.
