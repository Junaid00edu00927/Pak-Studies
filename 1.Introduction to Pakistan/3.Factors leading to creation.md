
## 1. Ideological & Religious Factors

### A. Two-Nation Theory (Do-Qawmi Nazriya)

* **Core Concept:** Muslims and Hindus in the Indian subcontinent were not merely two religious communities, but two distinct nations with fundamentally different religions, cultures, social values, philosophies, and historical traditions.
* **Sir Syed Ahmad Khan (Post-1857):** First articulated that Muslims form a separate nation requiring safeguards for their identity, particularly regarding education and language (Urdu-Hindi controversy of 1867).
* **Allama Iqbal’s Allahabad Address (1930):** Proposed the political consolidation of Muslim-majority provinces in North-West India into a separate state, providing the ideological foundation.
* **Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah:** Defined the nationhood of Muslims clearly during the **Lahore Resolution (1940)**, stating that Hinduism and Islam are two distinct social orders.

### B. Protection of Islamic Identity & Culture

* Fear of Hindu hegemony in a united democratic India governed strictly by majority rule (*"Ram Raj"*).
* Preservation of Islamic culture, religious freedom, Arabic/Persian script for Urdu, and family laws.

## 2. Political Factors & Key Events

### A. Formation of the All-India Muslim League (1906)

* Established at Dhaka to protect Muslim political rights and present their demands to the British government.

### B. Separate Electoralates (Minto-Morley Reforms, 1909)

* Granted Muslims the right to elect their own representatives through separate constituencies, giving official legal recognition to their distinct political identity.

### C. Congress Rule & Tyranny (1937–1939)

* Following the 1935 Government of India Act elections, the Indian National Congress formed provincial ministries.
* Policies such as the **Wardha Scheme**, mandatory singing of **Bande Mataram**, and political exclusion alienated Muslims, convincing them that co-existence under Congress leadership was unviable.
* **Day of Deliverance (Dec 22, 1939):** Observed by Muslims when Congress ministries resigned.

### D. The Lahore Resolution (March 23, 1940)

* Passed at Minto Park (now Iqbal Park), Lahore.
* Demanded that Muslim-majority areas in the North-Western and Eastern zones of India be grouped to constitute independent states.

---

## 3. Economic Factors

* **Economic Backwardness:** Under British rule and local Hindu money-lending systems (*baniya* system), Muslims were largely lagging behind in trade, commerce, and government employment.
* **Landlordism & Debt:** Muslim peasants in provinces like Bengal and Punjab were heavily indebted to Hindu moneylenders.
* **Demand for Economic Independence:** Muslims desired an independent state where they could control their own trade, financial policies, industrial expansion, and employment opportunities without majority dominance.

---

## 4. Educational and Social Factors

* **Aligarh Movement:** Initiated by Sir Syed Ahmad Khan, it modernised Muslim education, established M.A.O. College (later Aligarh Muslim University), and created a modern Muslim political and intellectual elite.
* **Urdu-Hindi Controversy (1867):** The Hindu demand to replace Urdu with Hindi (in Devanagari script) as the official court language in UP highlighted cultural divides.

---

## 5. The Final Push towards Partition (1945–1947)

| Event | Year | Historical Significance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **General Elections** | 1945–1946 | Muslim League won **100% of Central Muslim seats** and **86% of Provincial Muslim seats**, proving it was the sole representative of Indian Muslims. |
| **Cabinet Mission Plan** | 1946 | Offered a grouped federation; failed due to conflicting interpretations between League and Congress regarding provincial grouping. |
| **Direct Action Day** | Aug 16, 1946 | Called by Jinnah to demonstrate Muslim resolve for Pakistan, leading to widespread communal riots (e.g., Great Calcutta Killings). |
| **3rd June Plan** | 1947 | Lord Mountbatten announced the partition of British India into two independent dominions: India and Pakistan. |
| **Indian Independence Act** | July 1947 | Passed by British Parliament, officially creating Pakistan on **August 14, 1947**. |
